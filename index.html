Philosophy

How I Think About Marketing

Marketing that doesn't move a number isn't marketing, it's just activity.

I build programs around business outcomes: pipeline, conversion, cost efficiency, revenue. Every channel decision, content investment, and campaign structure traces back to what the business needs to grow.

I start with the buyer, map the strategy to their journey, and then execute with the same rigor I bring to the strategy itself.

I've worked across B2B and B2C, inside companies and as a consultant, with agencies, SDRs, product teams, ops, and executives. What stays consistent is the approach: be deliberate, be data-driven, and be accountable for the outcome, not just the output.

Strategy First

Every campaign starts with the business problem, the buyer, and the outcome. No channel gets funded without a clear reason.

Built to Execute

I own the work from brief to results. Strategy without execution is a document, not a program.

Measured by Revenue

MQLs matter. Pipeline matters more. I build shared KPI frameworks so marketing and sales are always pulling in the same direction.

B2B and B2C

From enterprise ABM programs to million-piece direct mail operations, I've run both sides of the marketing equation.

What is the difference between demand generation and ABM?
+
Demand generation is a broad, full-funnel approach to creating awareness and pipeline across a target market. Account-based marketing (ABM) is a focused strategy that targets specific high-value accounts with personalized messaging and coordinated sales and marketing outreach. Heather designs and executes both, often combining them into a tiered approach where ABM serves top-priority accounts and demand gen scales reach across the broader market.
How does Heather measure marketing success?
+
Heather measures success against business outcomes, not just activity metrics. Key measures include MQL-to-SQL conversion rates, pipeline influenced, cost per lead, lead-to-MQL conversion, and revenue contribution. She builds shared KPI frameworks between marketing and sales so both teams are measuring the same thing.
